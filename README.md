Defining A Temperature Scale | Mini Physics - Free Physics Notes
The lower fixed point is defined as the temperature at which pure ice melts at one atmospheric pressure, assigned the value of 0 °C.
Definition and Significance
The lower fixed point, often referred to as the ice point, is a critical reference point in temperature measurement. It is established at 0 °C (32 °F) and is used as a baseline for various temperature scales, including the Celsius scale. This point is significant because it is easily reproducible under standard atmospheric conditions, making it a reliable reference for calibrating thermometers.

Calibration of Thermometers
In the context of thermometer calibration, the lower fixed point is marked by immersing the thermometer bulb in pure melting ice. When the liquid in the thermometer stabilizes at this point, a mark is made, indicating the lower fixed point. This process ensures that the thermometer provides accurate readings at lower temperatures.

Comparison with Upper Fixed Point
The lower fixed point is often paired with the upper fixed point, which is the boiling point of water at one atmospheric pressure, assigned the value of 100 °C (212 °F). The interval between these two fixed points is divided into equal parts to create a temperature scale, such as the Celsius scale, which consists of 100 divisions, each representing 1 °C.

Conclusion
In summary, the lower fixed point is a fundamental concept in thermodynamics and temperature measurement, serving as a crucial reference for calibrating thermometers and establishing temperature scales. Its value of 0 °C is universally recognized and plays a vital role in scientific and practical applications of temperature measurement.
